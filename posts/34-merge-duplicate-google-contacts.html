How to Merge Duplicate Contacts in Google Contacts Without Losing Information

Published on July 31, 2026 • By Tweaknook Editorial

Duplicate contacts make names harder to search, split phone numbers and email addresses across separate records, and create confusing suggestions in Gmail or on a phone. Google Contacts can merge many duplicates automatically, but clicking Merge all without reviewing the records can combine people who share a name or preserve an outdated detail you meant to remove.

The safer approach is to confirm which Google Account owns the contacts, export a backup, review suggestions one at a time, and know how to reverse a mistake. The steps below cover Google Contacts on a computer and the Google Contacts app on Android. Menu names can change, and work or school accounts may have administrator restrictions.

Direct answer: In Google Contacts on a computer, open Merge & fix, review each suggested pair, and select Merge only when both records belong to the same person. Export your contacts first if the address book is important. Contacts stored in different Google Accounts cannot be merged directly.

Why Duplicate Contacts Appear

Duplicate records usually come from imports, phone migrations, multiple signed-in accounts, or saving the same person through different apps. They can also appear when one record contains a phone number and another contains an email address. Before merging, identify whether you have true duplicates or separate records that only look similar.

1. Confirm the Google Account Before You Merge

Many phones show contacts from several accounts in one combined list. A personal Google Account, a work account, a device-only address book, and a SIM card can all display at the same time. Google states that contacts saved in different Google Accounts cannot be merged.

On a computer, open Google Contacts and check the profile picture in the upper-right corner. On Android, open the Contacts app and use the account selector near your profile picture. If two records belong to different accounts, decide which account should keep the final contact before moving or importing anything.

Do not move company contacts into a personal account without permission. Work and school directories may be controlled by an administrator, and directory entries are different from contacts you created yourself.

2. Export a Backup Before a Large Cleanup

A backup is especially useful when you plan to merge many contacts, move records between accounts, or change auto-save settings.

  1. Open Google Contacts on a computer.
  2. Select the contacts you want to protect, or select all contacts.
  3. Open More actions and choose Export.
  4. Select Google CSV for a Google Contacts backup.
  5. Save the file in a private location you can identify later.

A contact export can contain names, phone numbers, email addresses, postal addresses, notes, and other personal information. Do not place the file in a public folder or share it through an unrestricted link. If you store the backup in Google Drive, organize it in a private folder and review access permissions using the checks in How to Organize Google Drive Without Disrupting Shared Files.

3. Review Duplicate Suggestions on a Computer

  1. Open Google Contacts.
  2. At the upper left, open the menu if it is collapsed.
  3. Select Merge & fix.
  4. Review each duplicate suggestion.
  5. Select Merge only when the records clearly belong to the same person.

If Google does not show a duplicate-merge option, it has no current suggestions for that account. This does not prove that every contact is unique; it only means Google is not offering a suggested merge at that time.

Use Merge all only after reviewing the list carefully. Pay particular attention to common names, family members who share a phone number, shared company inboxes, and old versus current employees. A matching name alone is not enough evidence.

4. Merge Specific Contacts Manually

Google may not suggest a pair when the records have different names or incomplete details. You can select the records manually:

  1. Return to the main contact list.
  2. Select the checkbox beside each record for the same person.
  3. Choose the merge action at the top of the page.
  4. Open the combined contact and review the result.

Check that the merged record contains the correct primary name, current phone number, current email address, organization, labels, notes, and important dates. Merging can preserve multiple values, so the result may still need editing. Remove only information you recognize as obsolete.

5. Merge Duplicates in the Google Contacts App on Android

On Android, Google's current instructions use the Contacts app:

  1. Open the Google Contacts app.
  2. Select the Google Account that contains the duplicates.
  3. Tap Fix & manage, then Merge & fix.
  4. Tap Merge duplicates.
  5. Review a suggestion and tap Merge, or use Merge all only after checking the list.

If the unwanted duplicates come from device storage, a SIM card, or another account, merging inside one Google Account may not remove what you see in the combined phone list. Filter the contact view by account to identify the source first. Google also notes that contacts saved to a Google Account sync across signed-in Android devices, so a merge may appear on other devices after synchronization.

6. Handle Contacts Stored in Different Accounts

Google Contacts cannot directly merge records stored in different Google Accounts. There are two safer options:

Move one contact on Android

The Google Contacts app can move a contact to another account. Google warns that moving a contact deletes it from the original account. Confirm the destination account and make a backup before moving important records.

Export and import on a computer

You can export selected contacts from one account and import the CSV or vCard file into another account. After confirming that the imported information is complete, use Merge & fix in the destination account. Do not delete the source contacts until you have checked names, phone numbers, email addresses, labels, and notes.

7. Check Other Contacts When Gmail Suggestions Look Duplicated

Google can automatically save people you interact with to Other contacts. These entries may appear as suggestions when you address a Gmail message even though they are not in the main contact list. Other contacts are available from Google Contacts on a computer, not from the regular mobile contact view.

Open Other contacts, review the entries, and either add useful records to your contacts or delete unwanted suggestions. You can also change the setting that controls whether Google automatically saves people you interact with. This is separate from Gmail filters; filters organize incoming messages rather than contact suggestions. For inbox organization, see How to Organize Gmail with Three Practical Filters.

8. Undo a Merge or Other Contact Changes

Google Contacts lets you undo contact changes made during the previous 30 days. This is a broad restore operation, not necessarily a one-contact undo. Restoring to an earlier time can also remove contacts added after that point from the current view.

  1. Open Google Contacts on a computer.
  2. Select the Settings icon.
  3. Choose Undo changes.
  4. Select the time you want to return to.
  5. Confirm the restore.

Export the current contacts before using Undo changes if you added or edited other important records after the chosen restore point. Contacts deleted permanently from Trash cannot be recovered through this process.

9. Prevent New Duplicates

  • Choose one primary Google Account for personal contacts.
  • Before saving a new contact, search by phone number and email address.
  • Review the account selected when adding a contact on Android.
  • Avoid importing the same CSV or vCard file repeatedly.
  • Review Other contacts if Gmail suggestions keep returning.
  • Keep one private backup before major imports or account moves.

If a contact list is large, clean it in small batches. Merge a group, verify the result on the web and phone, and then continue. This makes an incorrect combination easier to identify than merging hundreds of suggestions at once.

When You Should Not Merge

Do not merge records merely because their names match. Keep them separate when they represent different people, a person and a shared business address, separate work and personal identities you intentionally maintain, or records controlled by different organizations.

Stop and ask an administrator when the contacts belong to a managed work or school account and you are unsure who owns the records. Do not export an organizational directory or client list to a personal account without authorization.

The Practical Result

Duplicate-contact cleanup is safest when you separate three tasks: identify the account that owns each record, protect the current address book with an export, and review the merged result. Google's automatic suggestions can save time, but they do not replace checking the person's actual phone numbers, email addresses, and organization.

After the cleanup, verify a few important contacts on every device that syncs with the account. A smaller, accurate contact list is more useful than a fast merge that combines the wrong people or removes information you still need.
